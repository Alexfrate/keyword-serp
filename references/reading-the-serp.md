# Reading the SERP (real competition)

Organic competition is **who owns page 1** and **what kind of result** Google already chose. It is not the Planner Low/Medium/High dot.

## Signs of a weaker SERP (a query you can play)

- At the top: small sites, thin pages, old guides, the wrong intent (a tax article when you sell a shop).
- The wrong place (a Milan dentist on a Foggia query).
- Forums, weak aggregators, copied listicles.
- No Maps pack, or a pack of neglected listings, if you can be there.
- The top 3 do not answer the query in a closed way (price, hours, product).

## Signs of a hard SERP (drop it, or treat it as a hub with no expectations)

- Wikipedia, Amazon, Google itself, newspapers, trade directories repeating (Yellow Pages, Sortlist, G2).
- A 3-pack on Maps if you have no listing and no reviews.
- Ten known brands on a brand query (*Nike shoes*).
- "Best X in [country]" or "ranking" queries: Google wants lists, not your homepage.

High volume plus a hard SERP is not an opportunity. It is noise.

## What decides the format

Google has already picked the page type that "wins" that query. Match it.

| What you see in organic | Page to build | H1 |
|---|---|---|
| Guides, "how much", "how to" | Question page | The query, almost word for word |
| Product pages, grids | Product or category | Product or category name |
| Maps + reviews | Local listing (hours, area, name/address/phone) | Name + service + area, not an FAQ |
| Brand homepages | Home or catalog | Brand or offer, not a question |
| Comparison tables, G2 | Price list or "X vs Y" | As the SERP phrases it |

If the first eight results are products, a "what is X?" article is not low competition. It is the wrong format.

## Head term vs branches

A keyword group around one offer almost always has both:

- **Head term.** Lots of searches, hard SERP. Use it as the parent page if that is what you sell (*how much does a website cost*). It is not the easy branch.
- **Branches.** Same family, less volume, a weaker or more specific page 1 (*how much does a landing page cost*, *brochure site price*). This is where "low competition" actually is.

Pick the branches. If you also build the head term, do it on purpose: indexing and waiting, not magic.

## One URL, one intent

Two queries are the same intent when the SERP is almost the same. One page. Two queries with different SERPs (price vs tutorial vs local) need different URLs.

Clones and old URLs on the same intent: 301 them to the page you keep. Do not delete them if Google has already seen them.
